ii · strategy

The visual pivot

If every other Chinese hotpot brand in SG looks like a Chinatown postcard, we go the other way.
Moving away from

Chinatown
Postcard

  • Red lanterns · gold dragons · opera masks as decoration
  • Saturated red dominating 60%+ of every frame
  • Loud promotional energy — "限时优惠！" everywhere
  • Stock food photography lit like a menu board
  • Pagoda silhouettes treated as "Chinese-ness shortcut"
  • EN copy reads like Google Translate
→
Moving toward

Editorial
Heirloom

  • Brush calligraphy as the only ornament — chosen, not stacked
  • Deep green dominates · cream breathes · red is reserved for emphasis
  • Quiet copy — short Chinese sentences, like grandma talking
  • Cinematic food photography — chiaroscuro, steam, slow light
  • Botanical apothecary illustrations (jujube, goji, ginseng)
  • CN-led typography · EN sits beneath in italic Cormorant

The hierarchy is the secret. Most Chinese F&B brands use red as the BASE — we use it as the ACCENT. Deep green replaces red as the hero color, evoking traditional Chaoshan opera silks + porcelain inlay. Cream gives us editorial breathing room. The proportions matter more than the swatches.

字体规范

Type system · 4 families
CN BrushMa Shan Zheng
鲜养真
Used ONLY for hero calligraphy moments (1 character per frame). Imitates a chef's handwritten label. Never for body. Never for English.
CN DisplayNoto Serif SC 900
潮八八现切鸡煲
Brand mark, titles, posters. Bold but not aggressive. 字距 0.05em.
CN BodyNoto Sans SC 400 / 500
慢火细煮 · 锁住每一口的温度。从来不是大锅一炒,而是一只鸡, 一锅汤。
Captions, social copy, subtitles. Light to medium weight. line-height 1.7. Never goes bold.
EN Display + BodyCormorant Garamond + Inter
A bowl that does something.
Cormorant Garamond Italic for headlines. Inter Regular for body. Never use bold sans-serif for Chinese-style brands — it kills the mood.

摄影方向

Photography · 3 lighting recipes
Recipe 01
琥珀光 · Amber Key
Single warm window light, 45° from left. Hard shadows. Steam catches the rim light. Used for hero food shots — broth pours, cleaver close-ups.
Recipe 02
慢光 · Slow Light
Soft top light through linen diffusion. Even, dreamy. Used for the family table — multiple subjects, no harsh shadows on faces.
Recipe 03
夜色 · Night Mood
Deep amber, 80% shadow, single candle-like source. Used for "old world" cultural cutaways — herb close-ups, ingredient flat-lays.

视频字幕

Subtitle treatment · editorial caption

Reject centered-bottom karaoke subtitles. Instead: left-aligned editorial captions with italic pretitle + Chinese serif body + English supporting line. 88 stamp in top-right (replaces pagoda mascot). All 15 videos share this template.

v · directions
If "Modern Heirloom" isn't the right vibe

Two alternatives I'd happily explore

DIRECTION 01

Modern Heirloom

现代传家
What you're looking at. Editorial heritage — grandma's recipe box opened by an art director. Quiet authority, brush calligraphy as the only ornament, deep green dominant, red as accent. Best fit for the 温润食补 wellness audience.
References: Cereal magazine · 茶颜悦色 menus · Aman Tokyo brand book
DIRECTION 02

Folk Revival

潮汕文创
Lean HARDER into Chaoshan-specific folk culture — 嵌瓷 porcelain inlay patterns, 潮剧 opera silhouettes, lantern festival energy. Bolder, redder, more "museum gift shop". Strong if Joanna wants to position as cultural ambassador / tourist destination.
References: 故宫文创 · Shanghai Tang · Loong Mun Heen branding
DIRECTION 03

Apothecary Modern

中药铺
Push the wellness angle to the front. Sage greens + paper kraft + scientific botanical illustrations. The broth becomes the medicine, the restaurant becomes the modern apothecary. Best if expanding into bottled tonics / DTC product line.
References: Aesop · The Goodhood Store · 食养山房
